# A starter kit is only as good as what it refuses to add

## I made the foundation my design team builds prototypes on. The craft isn't the features I put in. It's the judgment about what stays out, so the next designer can build their own thing on top.

My team needed a place to start. Not a finished app to bend to a new idea, but a foundation to stand a new idea up on fast. So I made one, and the interesting part is not what it does. It is what I kept refusing to add. A starter kit stops being a launchpad the moment it becomes opinionated, so every feature had to earn its place by staying neutral enough that the next designer could build their own thing on top of it.

The clearest example is a persona system. Prototypes normally run on placeholder data, mock names and empty inboxes, so you never feel what the product does for a real person. Another team had built one and was still looking for somewhere it could land. I connected it to the kit, so a designer can pick a realistic synthetic persona and watch the whole prototype become that person: their greeting, their suggestions, their manager and files, even the assistant's search tools rerouted to that persona's data. It is genuinely useful, and it is exactly the kind of thing that could have quietly turned the kit into my app instead of a foundation.

So I held the line in the details. A tool that needs a live connection ships off by default and only appears when it's actually connected, instead of assuming everyone wants it. The persona system falls back to mock data so the kit runs with zero setup and no credentials. I pulled my own access token out of the flow so what my team inherits is clean. The avatar picker matches the nav patterns already there instead of introducing a new one. None of those are feature decisions. They are stewardship decisions, and every one of them answers the same question: does this keep the kit neutral enough for someone else to make it theirs.

Knowing what to leave out is harder than knowing what to put in. A foundation that tries to do everything is just someone else's finished product. The restraint is the design.
