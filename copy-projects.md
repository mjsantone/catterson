# A project that knows your work, and shows where it learned it

## Every product team was solving "projects" from inside its own app, so a small neutral team started from the work itself. I made a workspace that organizes itself from real work, catches you up, and acts for you, with a receipt on everything it claims.

A project here holds its goal, people, sources, decisions, and history, and anything you ask inside it starts from all of that. It exists because every product team was solving "projects" from inside its own app, content here, chat there, nobody owning the project itself. In July a handful of us were pulled together because we were neutral, and told to start from the unit of work. We made a repo the same day.

Home opens on cards made from a person's own work: requests, commitments, decisions waiting. Each says why it is there and where it came from. Ask what is new on something and the answer comes cited, with a project offered beneath it. Accept, and the files, chats, and meetings behind it fill in, each linked where it lives rather than copied. That was the first boundary I held. A reference is a pointer, not a copy, and not proof the system has read it.

Come back later and the project writes what changed, with a citation on every point. What it cannot verify it holds back, and says so. In September I split the prototype in two: what people see on top, and underneath a data layer that pulls commitments and decisions out of real sources, each with a receipt. The catch-up is that layer showing its work. A summary that is right most of the time teaches you to stop reading it.

Acting runs the same way. Ask for an email from the project's evidence and it drafts one to confirm. Say "make it shorter" and the same draft changes in place, with a note on what changed.

The strongest moment is the share. Products collapse three questions into one button: who is in the project, what is shared, and who can open the original file. The model keeps them apart. Sharing publishes a project's name and goal and nothing else. Each reference is a deliberate move, the original never changes who can open it, and every member keeps a private space inside the shared project. The dialog shows the project before and after, so what moves and what stays is plain before anything does. The first step is live against real storage; the rest is tested offline, its live rehearsal still ahead.

The team stayed small and the loops short: one-day points of view, parallel branches, a front end retired rather than maintained. My work is the boundary model, the catch-up, the act-and-revise contract, and the share model; the data pipelines and the component system already existed. Making a shared project private again is still open, and cost is not yet legible to the person paying for it. I would rather show those seams than paper over them.
