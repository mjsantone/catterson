# A project that already knows your work, and shows where it learned it

## Several product teams were each solving "projects" from inside their own app, so a small neutral team was asked to start from the work itself. I made a workspace that organizes itself from your real work, catches you up, and acts for you, with a receipt on everything it claims to know.

A matter, a launch, a deal runs for months, and every time you ask an assistant for help you rebuild the context by hand: the documents, who is involved, what was decided, how you want it done. Every product team saw this through its own lens. Content here, chat there. Nobody owned the project itself. In July a handful of us were pulled together because we were neutral, and told to start from the unit of work instead of the app. We made a repo the same day.

Home opens on cards made from your own work: requests to you, commitments you made, decisions waiting on you. Each says why it is there and links to where it came from. Beneath them, suggested projects name the workstreams already running through your files, chats, and meetings. Nothing was set up. The system inferred the work from the work.

Accept a suggestion, answer two questions, press Create. The project fills in with the real files, chats, and meetings behind it, each linked where it lives rather than copied, each moving from preparing to ready. Starting a project costs almost nothing, and nothing leaves the place it belongs. That was the first boundary I held. A reference is a pointer, not a copy, and not proof the system has read it.

Come back later and the project writes what changed since your last visit, with a citation on every point. What it cannot verify it holds back, and says so. This is the layer I split out in September: what people see on top, and underneath, a data layer that pulls commitments and decisions out of real sources, each with a receipt back to where it came from. The catch-up is that layer showing its work. A summary that is right most of the time teaches you to stop reading it. One that tells you what it does not know is one you can act on.

Acting runs the same way. Ask for an email or a meeting from the project's evidence and it drafts one for you to confirm. Say "make it shorter" and the same draft changes in place, with a note on what changed. The team stayed small and the loops short: one-day points of view, parallel branches, a competing front end retired rather than maintained. My work is the boundary model, the catch-up and its restraint, and the act-and-revise contract; the data pipelines and the component system already existed. The seams are plain. Moving a project from private to shared is designed and not yet made, and I argued it is the space worth the most. Cost is not yet legible to the person paying for it. I would rather show those than paper over them.
